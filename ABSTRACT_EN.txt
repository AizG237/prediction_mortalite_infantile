================================================================================
 ABSTRACT
 Determinants and Prediction of Infant Mortality in Cameroon:
 A Statistical and Machine Learning Analysis of the 2018 Demographic
 and Health Survey
================================================================================

INTRODUCTION
Sub-Saharan Africa bears a disproportionate share of the global infant
mortality burden, and Cameroon continues to register marked socioeconomic
and geographic disparities in child survival. Despite international
commitments under the Sustainable Development Goals, the modifiable
determinants of this outcome remain insufficiently quantified at national
scale. This study aimed to identify the key sociodemographic, reproductive,
and healthcare-access determinants of infant mortality among Cameroonian
women, and to develop interpretable machine learning tools deployable for
population-level risk stratification.

METHODS
Cross-sectional data were drawn from the 2018 Cameroon Demographic and
Health Survey (DHS, file CMIR71FL), comprising 14,677 women aged 15–49.
The binary outcome (Y = 1: at least one child death experienced) was
modelled using weighted binary logistic regression on 10,494 parous women,
with DHS sampling weights applied to account for the stratified two-stage
cluster design; multicollinearity was assessed via Variance Inflation
Factors (VIF). For prediction, six supervised classifiers were trained and
compared — Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost,
LightGBM, and CatBoost — using a stratified 75/25 train-test split,
5-fold stratified cross-validation, class-imbalance handling through
balanced weights, and SHAP-based interpretability analysis.

RESULTS
Infant mortality prevalence was 20.1% across the full sample and 28.1%
among parous women. All predictors passed the multicollinearity threshold
(maximum VIF = 6.73). Logistic regression identified early age at first
birth as the dominant risk factor (< 18 years: OR = 5.05; 18–19 years:
OR = 2.64), followed by no formal education (OR = 2.98), primary education
only (OR = 2.30), and poverty. Health facility utilization (OR = 0.77) and
current employment (OR = 0.81) emerged as protective factors. Among the
six ML classifiers, XGBoost achieved the highest performance (AUC = 0.889,
F1 = 0.643, Recall = 0.720); learning curves confirmed strong
generalizability with no evidence of overfitting. SHAP analysis corroborated
regression findings, ranking number of live births, maternal age, and
education level as the three most influential predictors. The full pipeline
was deployed as an interactive Streamlit web application for individual risk
estimation.

CONCLUSION
Early childbearing and low maternal education emerge as the most
policy-relevant and modifiable risk factors for infant mortality in Cameroon.
The convergence of statistical inference and machine learning findings
reinforces the robustness of these conclusions. Prioritizing girls'
education, delaying age at first birth, and expanding access to maternal
healthcare services — particularly in rural and economically disadvantaged
areas — are the primary public health interventions recommended to reduce
infant mortality disparities nationwide.
