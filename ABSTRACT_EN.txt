================================================================================
 ABSTRACT
 Determinants and Prediction of Infant Mortality in Cameroon:
 A Statistical and Machine Learning Analysis of the 2018 Demographic
 and Health Survey
================================================================================

BACKGROUND
Infant and child mortality remains a critical public health challenge in
sub-Saharan Africa, with Cameroon registering persistent disparities across
socioeconomic and geographic strata. Identifying modifiable determinants and
developing accurate predictive tools are essential to guide targeted
interventions and resource allocation.

OBJECTIVE
This study aimed to identify the sociodemographic, reproductive, and
healthcare-access determinants of infant mortality among Cameroonian women,
and to benchmark the predictive performance of classical and modern machine
learning classifiers on this outcome.

METHODS
Cross-sectional data were drawn from the 2018 Cameroon Demographic and
Health Survey (DHS), using the women's individual recode file (CMIR71FL).
The analytical sample comprised 14,677 women aged 15 to 49. The binary
outcome (Y = 1) was defined as having experienced the death of at least one
child. Sampling weights (V005) were applied throughout the statistical
analysis to account for the complex stratified two-stage cluster design.
For inference, a weighted binary logistic regression was fitted on the
10,494 parous women, with variables organized in three progressive blocks
(sociodemographic, reproductive, healthcare access) and multicollinearity
assessed by variance inflation factors (VIF, threshold > 10). For
prediction, six supervised classifiers were trained on a stratified 75/25
split and evaluated via 5-fold stratified cross-validation and a held-out
test set: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost,
LightGBM, and CatBoost. Class imbalance (ratio 3.98:1) was handled through
balanced class weights and scale_pos_weight. Model interpretability was
assessed via SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values.

RESULTS
Infant mortality prevalence was 20.1% overall and 28.1% among parous
women. No variable exceeded the VIF threshold (maximum VIF = 6.73),
confirming the absence of severe multicollinearity. Logistic regression
identified early age at first birth as the strongest risk factor (age < 18
years: OR = 5.05, 95% CI 4.11-6.21; age 18-19: OR = 2.64, 95% CI
2.13-3.28), followed by no formal education (OR = 2.98, 95% CI 2.04-4.34)
and primary education only (OR = 2.30, 95% CI 1.61-3.29). Poor and
very-poor wealth quintiles (OR = 1.59 and 1.48, respectively), higher
parity, and advanced maternal age were also significantly associated with
increased risk. Conversely, utilization of health facilities (OR = 0.77,
95% CI 0.69-0.86), current employment (OR = 0.81), health worker contact
(OR = 0.86), and residence in the Nord and Ouest regions were protective.
Among ML classifiers, XGBoost achieved the highest discriminative
performance (AUC = 0.889, F1 = 0.643, Recall = 0.720 on the test set),
closely followed by LightGBM (AUC = 0.889) and CatBoost (AUC = 0.887),
with negligible gaps between cross-validation and test AUCs confirming
model generalizability. SHAP analysis identified number of live births,
maternal age, household size, education level, and age at first birth as
the five most influential predictors, consistent with the logistic
regression findings.

CONCLUSIONS
This study demonstrates that early childbearing and low maternal education
are the dominant, policy-addressable risk factors for infant mortality in
Cameroon. The convergence of statistical and machine learning results
strengthens the validity of both approaches. XGBoost with SHAP
interpretability represents a robust, deployable framework for population-
level risk stratification that could inform community health worker
targeting and preventive intervention programs in high-burden regions.
